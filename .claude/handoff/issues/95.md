# #95 The level travels as a name, so a stale asset desyncs a room undetected

**state:** open  **labels:** bug, ready-for-agent

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Parent

#81 §7 — cut out of the loop/relay/flow audit.

## What to build

Snapshot coverage is otherwise sound: the packed form carries the tick, the RNG state, every player field and every object slot, and the AI, movement and player-pair objects hold nothing across ticks. The one simulation-relevant thing outside it is **the level**, which travels between clients as a *name* and is resolved by each client fetching it.

So a stale HTTP cache on one client, or an asset swapped mid-deploy while a match is running, gives two clients different ban maps under one name. Every collision, spawn and AI path then differs. The build-string check the relay already performs covers **code**, not assets, so nothing detects it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two clients in one room are guaranteed to be simulating the same ban map, or the disagreement is detected and reported rather than played through
- [ ] A client whose level asset differs from the room's is refused or repaired rather than left desyncing
- [ ] A deploy that swaps a level asset under a running match does not silently desync it
- [ ] The check covers the ban map specifically — the part the simulation reads — not merely the level's name
- [ ] A test drives two clients with differing level data and asserts the disagreement is caught

## Out of scope

- Shipping level data through the relay instead of by fetch, unless that turns out to be the smallest fix
- The build-string check, which covers code and is working as intended
- Custom or user-uploaded levels
